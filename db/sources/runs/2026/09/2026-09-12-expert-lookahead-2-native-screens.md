Expert Lookahead 2, native packages W2 (router-reuse forecast in shadow mode, C12, C13, forecast audit), the native half of W3 (twin calibration on the native forecasts) and W4 (native prefetch screens with two runtime revisions and two follow-up screens), executed against [[records/plan/expert-lookahead-2-replacement-router-reuse-memo-2026-09-11]] on the 48 GB M5 Pro. The offline packages are in [[sources/runs/2026/09/2026-09-11-expert-lookahead-2-offline-lab]]. Every arm here ran one engine process at a time from a fresh process, with the real preflight, under protocol `xla2-20260911` (20 GB profile, 4,255 slots, 25 GB preflight) or the diagnostic protocol `xla2-diag12-20260912` (12 GB profile, 1,313 slots) described below. Nothing was installed, published or committed; the installed 0.2.15 artifact (`8abb02b639285335b4fc3113819ebc6fe084bbf017fab2b4a52de3e471a551e3`) is unchanged. All artifacts live under `.build/expert-lookahead/xla2-20260911/` and `.build/expert-lookahead/xla2-diag12-20260912/` (ignored by git).

**Memory blocker and the diagnostic profile.** At the first native step the machine's reclaimable memory (`vm_stat` free plus purgeable plus file-backed) stayed near 18 GB, below the 20 GB profile's 25 GB preflight, because a virtual machine belonging to another running application held 11.2 GB; the plan forbids touching another task's process and forbids simulated availability, and a 45-minute readiness watch timed out. The W2 shadow capture therefore ran under a separately prepared diagnostic protocol, `Tools/expert_lookahead.py prepare --binary .build/arm64-apple-macosx/release/slotstream --memory-gb 12 --run-id xla2-diag12-20260912 --successor-of .build/expert-lookahead/xla-pilot-20260911 --eligibility process-pageins-v1 --eligibility-approval "<plan approval quote>"` (`protocol.json` SHA-256 `94fc7332a43b341240471f369f771ad23a9eaf4125c55bde573fc6788b30adbb`, binary candidate 1, 12 GB profile with a 17 GB preflight that was satisfied, same pinned environment, corpus and request sets as the frozen protocol). Forecast agreement, recall and exactness do not depend on the slot count; miss coverage and non-resident precision do, and are labeled with their profile. **Twin against native (the W4 calibration check).** Per verification pass, the twin's chosen setting issues 358.0 tickets and counts 215.1 timely adoptions against 329.9 misses (precision 0.601, coverage 0.652, 19.8% of decode credited). Native F2r: 364.0 issued (+1.7%), 207.2 adopted (-3.7%), 326.8 misses per pass in the off arm (-0.9%), precision 0.569, 63.4% of demand records removed, 19.6% of decode saved on the demand IO clock. Every count and the IO credit are within the plan's 10%. The read-cost model also reproduces the measured demand-IO time of every arm within 3 s (events at 48 per pass times 0.3836 ms plus records times 0.2099 ms): control arms 52.0 s modeled against 51.2 to 51.7 s measured; prefetch arms 25.8 against 22.9 s (F2), 23.4 against 24.6 s (F2 revised), 30.9 against 31.7 s (stride 1 thresholded), 32.4 against 29.9 s (stride 2 thresholded); the per-record rise in the prefetch arms (0.263 ms to 0.296 ms at stride 2) is the model's fixed cost per layer event spread over fewer records, not contention. The loss is entirely in the non-IO part of decode: control 73.1 s against 154.2 s on F2 (+81.1 s), 65.6 against 105.6 s on F2 revised (+40.0 s), 72.8 against 102.8 s at stride 1 thresholded (+30.0 s), 72.6 against 100.6 s at stride 2 thresholded (+28.0 s). Host counters attribute joins on in-flight tickets (29.7, 13.0, 7.7, 0.0 s), adoption enqueue (29.8, 13.5, 7.8, 0.1 s) and the forecast (2.9% of the control decode, from F1); the remainders are 18.2, 10.4, 11.0 and 24.3 s and no host counter names them. The twin counted 97.6% of the native stride-1 adoptions as timely because its service model serves each ticket independently, and it charges nothing for the runtime's work on the critical path. The stride-2 follow-up isolates that term: promotions fell to 2.1%, join and adoption enqueue to 0.15 s over twelve arms, demand IO fell from 51.2 s to 29.9 s as modeled, and the non-IO part of decode still grew 28.0 s, 24.3 s of it unattributed. The adoption path does per-record work the demand path does not (a concatenation of each ticket's own buffers before the pool scatter, one array wrapper with a finalizer per piece per ticket, one 2.76 MB allocation and release per ticket, including the 76,188 tickets that expired unused at stride 2); which of these carries the 24 s is a profiling question, not answered by these runs.

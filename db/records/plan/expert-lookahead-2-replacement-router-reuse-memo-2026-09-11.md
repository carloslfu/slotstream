**Status: proposed, awaiting Carlos's approval. Nothing in this plan has run natively; no code path described as planned exists unless the code map says it does. It is the successor to [[records/plan/expert-lookahead-local-experiment-2026-09-10]], which closed at its offline continuation rule, and it stands on that experiment's exact capture, calibrated replay and proven prefetch runtime plus the three probes in [[records/measurements/expert-lookahead-router-reuse-and-replacement-probes-2026-09-11]]. No predictor is trained on the main path.**

### 1. Objective, scope and fixed decisions

Reduce the exposed expert-read time of MTP text decode on the existing Slotstream checkpoint by three exact mechanisms, in dependency order: a better slot replacement policy, prefetch driven by the model's own routers evaluated one and two layers early, and an early-layer routing memo keyed by token identity. Exactness is not negotiable: the chosen baseline's routing, expert bytes, numerical execution and outputs stay identical, so every mechanism only changes which bytes are in memory when. Levers that change arithmetic (dropping low-weight missing experts, lower-precision records, changed draft depth) are outside this plan; they are product decisions, not engineering steps.

The experiment runs on the existing 48 GB M5 Pro with its internal SSD. Rental budget is zero. The primary profile is the one the evidence was measured in: 20 GB target, 25 GB reclaimable preflight, 4,255 global slots (about 89 experts per layer), where demand reads take 38.7% of decode time. A 24 GB cohort (29 GB preflight, 5,702 slots, reads about 34% of wall time per [[records/measurements/decode-wall-time-attribution-2026-09-10]]) is a separately frozen experiment with a smaller ceiling, run only when the machine offers the headroom; results are never pooled across profiles.

Fixed decisions carried from the previous protocol: text mode, MTP at the adopted depth two ([[records/decisions/draft-depth-defaults-to-two]]), `SLOTSTREAM_DRAFT_DEPTH=2` pinned in every arm with adaptive speculation and draft-tail shortening off, prefill chunk 256, prefix retention off, elastic resizing off, greedy seed 42, thinking off, maximum context 32,768, the current resolved deployment optimizations, and the public 307-request corpus with its seed-1729 family split (train 242, validation 32, test 33 requests; test families sealed). The offline dataset is the pilot capture `xla-pilot-20260911` (69 requests, 12,899 outputs, 5,205 verification passes, 4.40 GB of shards): no new capture is required for any offline package, and the 50k campaign of the previous plan is retired.

The learned whole-pass predictor, its trainer and its packs are retired from the main path. The only trained component this plan admits is a small reuse-distance scorer for eviction, conditional, offline-only, and entered only if implementable policies stall (Section 5). "No training" is a property of the primary path, not a rule that forbids the conditional package.

Win criterion, unchanged from the previous plan: at least 10% repeatable native throughput improvement at equal total memory with exact correctness gates passing, on the sealed held-out B0 cohort. Each mechanism also has its own smaller gate so that a partial result is a complete outcome. No installed executable replacement, default activation, publication or release is part of this proof.

**Ceiling arithmetic.** Use the measured read-cost model, not a proportional one. Over the 1,025 validation passes decode took 233.6 s, of which demand reads 90.4 s; the fitted cost splits those reads into a fixed part `F` (0.38 ms for each of the 49,200 layer events with a miss, 18.9 s) and a marginal part `M` (0.21 ms for each of the 338,147 missing records, 71.0 s). A replacement policy multiplies the record count by `m`; timely prefetch hides a fraction `c` of the marginal part and hides a layer's fixed part only when it covers every miss of that layer, which is ignored below as a conservative simplification; `o` is new exposed overhead as a fraction of decode. Then `decode' = 143.2 + 18.9 + m * (1 - c) * 71.0 + o * 233.6` seconds and `ratio = 233.6 / decode'`. Hiding every read gives 1.63x. Replacement alone at the optimal bound (`m = 0.50`) gives about 1.18x; at an illustrative `m = 0.75` about 1.08x (the best implementable policy measured so far is `m = 0.92`, about 1.03x). Stride-1 top-10 prefetch alone (`c = 0.49` from the twin) projects 1.17x (the previous plan's proportional formula, which lets prefetch hide the fixed part too, says 1.23x). Combined with 2% overhead, `m = 0.75, c = 0.5` projects about 1.21x and `m = 0.5, c = 0.5` about 1.27x. These are conditional projections under an idealized service model that ignores contention from wasted reads; native measurement decides. No gain is guaranteed, every number above is smaller at 24 GB, and a proportional formula overstates replacement because halving misses does not halve the fixed cost.

### 3. Principles the pilot established

1. **Counts offline, time native.** The twin reproduces native cache decisions exactly, so any replacement policy or admission rule is scored offline with no fidelity loss in seconds. The twin's timing projection is optimistic in four known ways: it ignores bandwidth contention from wasted reads; it serves speculative reads on eight lanes at the measured 0.27 ms each, about 34 records inside a 1.16 ms lead, whereas the demand path's marginal rate is about 4.8 records per ms; it keeps the recorded baseline deadlines instead of advancing them as stalls disappear (the previous plan's causal event simulation requirement, carried into W3 as a sensitivity report); and it releases cap bytes at the demand check rather than at scatter release. It was contradicted natively once (a 2.6% precision policy at roughly 4x traffic decoded 2x slower). Throughput is therefore only ever claimed from paired native arms, and W4's calibration gate reads native counts against these optimisms.
2. **The free predictor comes first.** Routing at layer T is a linear readout of T's residual, and the residual changes by one bounded sublayer per step; the router applied one sublayer early is the strongest zero-cost forecast available. A learned correction on the same input could add to it, which is why learned components enter only as a measured improvement over that baseline, never instead of it.
3. **Misses are the target.** Every policy is scored on timely miss coverage, precision among issued non-resident candidates, and traffic amplification, never on routing recall alone.
4. **The read-cost model is the objective.** A layer with any miss pays about 0.4 ms plus about 0.2 ms per record. Replacement and prefetch are both scored by the read time they remove under this model, and zero-miss layer events are reported separately.
5. **Retire, do not delete.** The trainer, pack export and predictor-pack policies stay in the repository as historical instruments; this protocol does not run them. The collector, the ticket runtime, the twin and the paired bench are the instruments it does run.

### 5. Replacement policy: offline lab, then native port

**Why first.** It costs no read bandwidth, cannot change outputs, and is evaluated exactly offline in seconds per policy, so it is the cheapest lever to prove or kill. Its ceiling at 4,255 slots is a hit rate of 84.8% against 69.3%.

**Candidates.** CLOCK (reference, ships). Exact LRU (implementable, but within a point of CLOCK; kept as a reference). Segmented LRU with a 20% probation segment. S3-FIFO (small FIFO, main FIFO with lazy promotion, ghost queue). W-TinyLFU (1% window LRU, segmented main, count-min sketch admission with periodic halving). ARC (adaptive recency/frequency lists with ghost lists). CLOCK-Pro (the CLOCK approximation of LIRS, the natural upgrade for a CLOCK engine). Each policy is implemented once in the twin with `ensureCore`'s batch semantics: all keys of a layer event are pinned before victims are chosen, victims are chosen in demand order, and no key of the current batch is evicted. Constants (segment sizes, sketch width, ghost length) are fit on the train split's requests and selected on validation; report the train-prefix curve to expose overfitting. Recency state is seeded from the recorded prefill admission order (collector kind 6) and the snapshot's reference bits rather than from slot order, so recency policies are not handicapped at the start of each request as they were in the probe; report both seedings.

**Scoring.** For each policy at the native slot count and at 1.2x: misses versus native, hit rate, projected read time under the read-cost model, zero-miss layer events, and the per-layer miss profile. Report Belady's optimal beside every table. Also score each policy at the 24 GB slot count as a projection only.

**Layer-aware objective.** Because a layer with any miss pays the fixed cost, test two variants of the best policy: a per-layer quota that protects layers whose miss rate is already low, and victim preference for layers that already have a miss in the current pass. These are scored by read time, not miss count.

**Conditional learned evictor (W1c).** Only if the best implementable policy stays above 0.85x native misses on validation: a reuse-distance scorer with per-key features (passes since last use, count in the last 8, 32 and 128 passes, layer, expert frequency rank) and a logistic or small tree model trained on train-split traces to predict reuse within the next 16 passes, used to choose among a bounded sample of unpinned slots near the hand. It is trained in minutes on the existing shards, exported as a small table or weight vector, and ported only if it reaches the same 0.85x port threshold on validation and beats the best implementable policy by at least three hit-rate points. This is the only trained component in the plan.

**Native port.** Implement the winner behind the policy seam, default CLOCK, selectable by `SLOTSTREAM_EXPERT_REPLACEMENT=<policy>` registered as a validated optimization control. Requirements: victim selection amortized O(1) with bounded scans, per-reference bookkeeping on the model thread, which is the pool's only serialization, ghost state bounded to at most two keys per slot, no wall-clock inputs, and identical behavior in `ensureCore`, ticket adoption and sweep admission. Exactness proof: a native capture with the policy on must replay 100% in the twin (victims and miss lists), as CLOCK did; any mismatch blocks the port. Outputs are compared exactly against the CLOCK arm (C06 on the six correctness requests).

**Interplay with [[records/decisions/clock-stays-the-eviction-policy]].** The decision's reversal condition is an implementable policy beating CLOCK by more than about one point on a second workload. Segmented LRU already does so by 2.4 points in the exact offline replay of the pilot corpus, so the literal condition is met; this plan sets a stricter bar before the shipped policy changes: the 0.85x port threshold, the 100% replay proof and a measured native gain. The reversal is recorded as a new decision linking this plan's measurement, and it notes that the agentic many-short-turns workload the original decision named remains unmeasured.

### 6. Router-reuse forecast and prefetch

**Forecast.** At the completed-layer boundary of layer `l`, for strides `s` in the configured list (default 2 then 1) and targets `T = l + s`, run target T's own hyper-connection read on the live streams and T's router, take the top-`c` per position (default `c = 10`), and record each candidate's margin (its logit minus the row's tenth logit). Candidates whose margin is at or above the threshold, unioned across the three positions and deduplicated against resident keys and live tickets, are issued up to the per-target cap. Stride 2 carries the bulk (5.5 ms of lead, enough for a layer's misses by the demand-batch cost model); stride 1, whose 1.16 ms lead fits about four records by that same model (`(1.16 - 0.38) / 0.21`), refreshes target `l + 1` with the better forecast and can add candidates the stride-2 pass missed; it never cancels tickets already issued. Because the scheduler's issue cursor is monotone today, the refresh requires the dirty-target re-scan named in Section 4; without it the stride-1 candidates would silently never be read.

**Admission and traffic.** The read-cost model and the SSD's idle share bound how much waste is affordable: demand reads occupy about 39% of decode, so speculative reads that land inside compute windows are free up to roughly the same volume again, and the ticket runtime already defers speculative lane acquisition while demand reads run. Offline, choose `c`, the margin threshold, the per-target cap and the strides on validation to maximize the read-cost projection under an amplification allowance of 2.0x, reporting the whole frontier. The allowance is untested: the pilot's only native traffic evidence is the 2x slowdown at roughly 4x traffic, so W3 must report the amplification of the default stride union (which the twin has not run yet) and W4's calibration must relate measured traffic to measured throughput before any setting above 1.5x is trusted. Natively, the shadow arm measures forecast cost and the prefetch arm measures the truth. If the native gain is absent while the twin projects it, the diagnosis order is: issued and adopted counts against the projection, lane deferrals, promotion joins, adoption seconds, then read contention.

**Memory.** The router policy needs no pack. The reserve is the staging cap: keep the 32-record cap and 128 MiB reserve for the first native screen so F2 stays comparable with the pilot's B1 arms, then test a 16-record cap with a 64 MiB reserve, which returns about 24 slots to the pool at equal total memory.

**Exactness.** The forecast is an observer of the completed state and a producer of candidate lists; the real router, expert kernels and outputs are untouched. C06 (arbitrary correct or wrong forecast lists cannot change outputs) already exists and is rerun with the router policy. A new self-check (C12) runs the forecast function on the true post-attention streams of layer T and requires exact agreement with T's real router IDs on the correctness requests; it proves the mixing and router path is the model's own.

**Overhead.** The forecast adds, per layer, two hyper-connection reads and two router matmuls on three rows and one host materialization. Budget: at most 2% of decode in the shadow arm (F1), measured against the reserve-only control, the same budget the previous plan set for predictor plus scheduler overhead. If it exceeds the budget, first batch the strides into one graph and one sync, then drop stride 1.

**Early layers.** Layer 0 has no source and layer 1 only stride 1; layers 1 to 3 forecast at 0.43. This is where the memo of Section 7 applies, and until it does, those layers use ordinary demand loading.

### 7. Early-layer token memo

Per request, keep a bounded table from token ID to the observed top-10 experts at each of the first `M` layers (default `M = 8`, zero-based 0 to 7), updated from the real router IDs at those layers for each evaluated position's token, and used at the start of every verification pass to seed candidates for those layers from the pending token and the two drafts. The keys exist only once the drafts do, a few milliseconds before the pass begins, so the lead runs from pass begin: about one attention sublayer (1.2 ms) at layer 0 and about 4.6 ms more for each later layer, roughly 35 ms at layer 7. That is enough for every memo layer but the first, provided the memo has its own pass-start issue path: `beginPass` today issues only the first window of layers, so memo candidates for layers beyond the window would otherwise wait for their tick and lose most of the lead. The memo issues in layer order at pass start under its own share of the record cap (at most half), so it cannot starve the router candidates of the first window, and a cap refusal on a memo candidate does not block the router path. The table is exact observed labels, not a trained object; it is cleared at request end and bounded to 4,096 tokens (about 1.3 MB at ten IDs per layer), evicting least recently used entries.

Offline first: `Tools/expert_lookahead_probes.py memo` replays the memo over the validation shards and reports, per layer, miss coverage, precision among non-resident candidates and amplification, then the twin projects it with candidates handed over at pass begin against the recorded per-layer deadlines. Gate for native work: at least 4% of all misses covered in time at precision 0.30 or better, which is about 1.01x alone by the read-cost model; the memo enters B0 only if the combined twin projection improves by at least 0.01 with it. Natively, the memo is a candidate source of the same scheduler, on only with `SLOTSTREAM_EXPERT_PREFETCH_MEMO_LAYERS > 0`, exact under C06, and measured as a third arm on the screen. If the offline gate fails, record the numbers and drop the package; a bigram-keyed variant is an extension, not a fallback in this protocol.

Reuse C01 to C10 of the previous plan where they apply, and add:

Memory rules are those of `AGENTS.md` and the previous plan: inspect the model lock and process inventory before every heavy step, never stop another task's process, check actual reclaimable memory, one model process at a time, kill test processes when done and verify with `pgrep`, no cache purge, memory hog or simulated availability, never lower the minimum slot floor. Offline packages need no model process and run under an 8 GB physical-footprint ceiling. Native arms need the 25 GB preflight at 20 GB (29 GB at 24 GB) and at most 600 s of waiting per launch.

**Eligibility rule for timing (needs approval).** The previous rule required host swap-in and swap-out counters stable for 120 s before an arm and unchanged during it; during the pilot's native pairs swap-ins moved continuously with other applications and no pair qualified, although earlier clean pairs on this Mac (the wall-time attribution and the 0.2.15 reruns) did satisfy it. Under [[records/decisions/global-paging-is-diagnostic]] this protocol freezes, before any timing arm and only with Carlos's explicit approval: readiness is thermal nominal, low power off, reclaimable memory at or above the preflight, and host swap-outs stable over a 30 s sample; a pair is timing-eligible when both arms complete with exact outputs, no host swap-outs occur during either arm, the engine process's own page-ins (a new per-process counter, see the bench row of Section 4) grow by at most 4,096 pages during the measured request, and thermal state is nominal afterwards. Swap-ins are dropped from readiness because they are other processes reading back their own pages and cannot be attributed; swap-outs are kept because they mean active pressure that can evict the engine's pages. The page-in counter is process-attributed (`proc_pid_rusage`, which also counts file-backed faults, at this machine's 16 KiB page size, so 4,096 pages is 64 MiB), and because MLX maps the resident weights, W0 measures the engine's own page-ins over a clean 512-output request before the threshold is frozen; the 4,096 figure is a placeholder until then. Both the old and the new verdict are recorded per pair; the headline uses the new rule; excluded rows are preserved. If Carlos does not approve the change, the old rule stands and a cohort that cannot qualify is recorded as inconclusive after its finite attempts.

### 9. Native benchmark and decision protocol

Arms use one frozen candidate binary with controls off and on, the off path verified against the pristine baseline. Comparisons, reusing the pilot's generators and drivers:

- **R1 (replacement):** CLOCK versus the ported policy at the same slot count and total budget; no prefetch in either arm.
- **F1 (forecast overhead):** reserve-only control versus router policy in shadow mode (C13).
- **F2 (prefetch mechanism):** both arms charge the reserve; prefetch off versus router prefetch on, CLOCK in both.
- **B0 (headline):** current deployment versus the frozen combination (policy, prefetch, memo where admitted) at the same total budget.

The validation screen is the previous plan's: two frozen prompts each from code, reasoning and prose, two paired rounds, 128 measured outputs after the 128-output warmup, 12 pairs, at least one clean pair per prompt, aggregate median-pair gain of at least 3% to proceed. The held-out B0 cohort is unchanged: six sealed test families, two frozen prompts per family, three preplanned paired rounds, 36 pairs and 72 arms, 512 measured outputs, alternating arm order, equal family weights, geometric-mean aggregation, 10,000 bootstrap draws with seed 1729, at least two clean pairs per prompt, no replacement pairs. Any output mismatch is a correctness failure of the candidate regardless of timing eligibility.

**Initial proof passes only when** all relevant checks pass, the B0 aggregate ratio is at least 1.10, the lower bootstrap bound is above 1.00, no family median ratio is below 0.95 and no family median request duration regresses by more than 5%. A 1.03 to 1.10 aggregate is recorded as a small measured benefit that does not close the gate; a per-mechanism arm may pass its own gate while the combination does not.

### 11. Conditional extensions

| Extension | Entry condition | Required additional work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forecast-protected eviction | Prefetch works but a measurable share of misses are keys the forecast named for the next two layers and CLOCK evicted meanwhile. | Let the policy treat active forecast targets as referenced; ablate against the plain policy at equal memory; no permanent pins. |
| Union of strides and shorter refresh | Stride 1 adds coverage but its 1.16 ms lead wastes reads. | Issue stride-1 candidates only when the lane budget is idle; measure separately. |
| GPU-resident forecast | The per-layer host materialization is the dominant forecast cost. | Keep candidates on the GPU and read them at the next boundary with a one-layer delay; requires the GPU slot lookup of the previous plan's extension table. |
| Bigram or n-gram memo | The token memo passes its gate and later-layer misses remain token-driven. | Key the memo by the last two tokens, reusing the n-gram hashing precedent of `NgramStore`; measure repeat rate against precision. |

| Learned reuse-distance evictor beyond W1c | W1c ports and the remaining gap to Belady is still large. | Richer features, per-layer models, cost measured per eviction. |

Keep the original pack and existing allocation as the supported defaults. Do not advertise twenty committed generation tokens per second across the full sixteen-to-sixty-four-GB Mac range from the current evidence. The full goal stays open; this is a practical result for tested recipes, not a proof that every possible quantization or future kernel is impossible.

The active plan and [[records/measurements/quantization-screen-2026-10-02]] preserve the complete comparisons. Original at the Desktop ceiling clears the target on the three declared workloads on the owned Mac. Both original and native-three-bit miss at fourteen and twenty-two GB. The clean ten-GB original pilot is around nine to ten tokens/s; neither the native-three-bit draft nor plain recipe gives the required useful improvement across the existing workloads. A smaller prefill reservation at fourteen GB adds cache but materially slows longer requests. Lower-bit mixed affine and mixed VQ miss their separately frozen completed-task gates. Fewer stored or requested bytes and restored cache hits have not reliably produced better complete requests.

Close the tested allocation cap, low-budget three-bit draft recipe and its one plain-decode follow-up. Preserve the incomplete thirty-three-GB candidate allocation attempts without calling them passes or losses. That candidate-only configuration is not required to close an unmet recommendation where the original already meets the tested target. Reopen it if an independently useful candidate earns further qualification, or if a new measured mechanism makes it relevant to the missed budgets. Do not spend repeated quiet-host retries on it now.

Retain both independent automatic systems and user overrides. Their infrastructure and original-pack lifecycle are implemented, but the useful alternative quantization recommendation set remains incomplete: no tested alternative earns production Auto admission. Never populate that set merely to claim completion. Do not weaken installation authentication, activation/rollback, saved ceilings or request-boundary rules to compensate for a performance miss.

The available choices are explicit:

- Keep the same checkpoint and publish measured speed tiers, with the twenty-token claim limited to its tested configurations and lower budgets honestly slower.
- Keep the full target open for a materially new quality-preserving low-bit or kernel/streaming mechanism. Require a cheap positive screen and a specific plausible contribution to the remaining gap before implementation or another large export. This is further research with an uncertain outcome, not a delivery promise.
- If the owner changes the same-checkpoint constraint, qualify a smaller model for the lower-memory range while preserving explicit user control. Do not make that change silently, and do not assume a different model meets the target without testing it.

Only the owned forty-eight-GB Mac is required for validation. Reduced process budgets retain its physical RAM and OS file-cache advantages; other Macs' speed remains estimated or unknown. No additional hardware purchase or validation campaign is made a prerequisite.

The October 6 mechanism round is also closed. The upstream fused QMM screen in [[sources/runs/2026/10/2026-10-05-fused-qmm-component-screen]] gives no useful gain over ordinary batched MLX at the deployed three rows. Ordinary batching removes reference overhead but fails the unchanged native plain/speculative output invariant in [[sources/runs/2026/10/2026-10-06-native-batched-invariance-failure]]. The narrower fixed-row alternative passes its clean component criterion and every native lifecycle assertion, but its complete fourteen-GB pilot in [[sources/runs/2026/10/2026-10-06-fixed-row-native-and-performance-screen]] is only about one percent faster on short/context and slower on coding than the original medians. It misses the frozen gain criterion and is reverted. Preserve all failures, the timing-excluded preliminary component run and the clean retry. Do not loosen quality, equality or timing gates, repeat these unchanged recipes or promote an empty recommendation set.

The implementation and feasibility gap remains explicit: original-pack automatic selection, independent saved ceilings, runtime adjustment and transactional lifecycle are implemented and tested, but a useful alternative recommendation and winner-specific installation/release cannot be finished by admitting a losing pack. Current tested options do not support the full speed promise. Keep the same-checkpoint goal open, with the options above, and start further implementation only after a materially new evidence-backed mechanism earns a bounded experiment.
